# Lab 3 - Analyze BP-style operational data with Copilot in Excel

[← Previous: Lab 2](lab-02-word.md) | [Back to Home](../index.md) | [Next: Lab 4 →](lab-04-powerpoint.md)

## Instructor notes

Before starting, remind learners that data quality affects Copilot output. Point out that Copilot can identify patterns and suggest questions, but it should not replace engineering, finance, safety, or operational analysis. Encourage learners to ask Copilot to explain assumptions and limitations.

## Scenario

Imagine you are reviewing monthly operating metrics for BP retail stations, terminals, and refinery units. The sample workbook contains fields such as Month, Site, Region, Product, Throughput, Maintenance Hours, Safety Observations, Energy Consumption, Emissions Estimate, and Customer Satisfaction Score.

## Lab steps

1. Open the sample Excel workbook.
2. Select Copilot in Excel.
3. Enter the prompt:

   > Summarize the dataset and provide an overview of key operational, safety, energy, and customer metrics.

4. Prompt Copilot:

   > Create a table showing the key patterns in throughput, maintenance hours, safety observations, emissions estimate, and customer satisfaction by region.

5. Prompt Copilot:

   > Show a line chart of monthly throughput and emissions estimate. Highlight any months where throughput increased while emissions also increased.

6. Prompt Copilot:

   > Identify any outliers or anomalies in safety observations and maintenance hours, and suggest possible business questions to investigate.

7. Ask Copilot to summarize findings:

   > Create a management-ready summary with three insights, two risks, and three recommended follow-up actions.

## Debrief questions

- What trends did Copilot identify?
- Were any insights surprising or unsupported?
- Which additional fields would improve the analysis, such as downtime reason, asset class, permit status, or contractor availability?
