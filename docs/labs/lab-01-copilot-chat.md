# Lab 1 - Work smarter with Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat for BP operations

[← Back to Home](../index.md) | [Next: Lab 2 →](lab-02-word.md)

## Instructor notes

Use this lab to introduce the difference between broad prompts, grounded prompts, and refinement prompts. Emphasize that web-style or general answers must not be treated as BP policy. Encourage learners to ask Copilot for structure, options, and questions rather than final operational decisions.

## Scenario

Imagine you are a business operations analyst supporting BP’s downstream and mobility leadership team. You need to prepare a concise briefing on refinery performance, retail energy demand, safety priorities, and customer trends before a weekly leadership huddle. In this lab, you use Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat to research, brainstorm, summarize, and structure business insights for an oil and gas audience.

## Lab steps

1. Open Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat.
2. Enter the prompt:

   > Summarize three current business priorities for a global oil and gas company like BP, focusing on operational reliability, safety, and lower-carbon transition. Present the answer as briefing notes for a senior leader.

3. Ask a follow-up prompt:

   > Create five practical questions that a BP operations leader should ask during a weekly refinery and retail performance review.

4. Prompt Copilot to refine the output:

   > Rewrite the briefing in a concise executive tone, with separate sections for Safety, Operations, Customers, and Energy Transition.

5. Review the response and identify which points require validation using internal BP-approved sources.

## Debrief questions

- Which prompt produced the most useful executive briefing?
- What information should be validated against BP-approved sources?
- How could the prompt be improved for a refinery, retail, trading, or mobility audience?
